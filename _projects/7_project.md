## Overview

Our research in Remote Sensing focuses on developing robust and efficient deep learning methods
for understanding complex changes and structures in Earth-observation imagery. A major
direction is multi-temporal change detection, where we investigate state-space models such as
Mamba for capturing long-range spatial and temporal dependencies while preserving
fine-grained boundaries. This work has led to methods for both binary and semantic change
detection, including Mamba-FCS, which combines spatio-frequency feature fusion,
change-guided attention, and class-imbalance-aware learning to improve the detection of subtle
and rare land-cover transitions. Our research also explores remote-sensing semantic
segmentation under domain shift, studying how modern visual state-space backbones generalize
across changing geographic environments and where boundary-related failures arise. Beyond
discriminative modelling, we investigate generative AI for Earth observation, including
prompt-controlled diffusion models that synthesize realistic satellite imagery with controllable
land-cover composition to mitigate long-tail class imbalance. Together, these efforts aim to
build accurate, scalable, and robust AI systems for applications including land-cover
monitoring, urban development analysis, environmental monitoring, and disaster assessment.
