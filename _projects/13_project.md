Unlike conventional RGB imagery, this allows materials with similar visual appearances to be

used in applications such as remote sensing, mineral mapping, precision agriculture,
environmental monitoring, and defence.

A major challenge, however, is that the limited spatial resolution of hyperspectral sensors
causes many pixels to contain mixtures of several materials. Hyperspectral Unmixing (HSU)
aims to identify these constituent materials, known as endmembers, and estimate their
proportional contributions, or abundances, within each pixel. Our project investigates HSU with
particular attention to spectral variability, changes in material signatures caused by
illumination, atmospheric conditions, surface topography, shadows, and intrinsic material
variations. Understanding and compensating for these variations is essential for developing
accurate and robust hyperspectral analysis methods for real-world environments.
