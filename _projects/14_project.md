## Overview

Sociohydrology studies the two-way relationship between water systems and society—how climate
and hydrological processes influence people, agriculture, settlements, and water management,
and how human decisions in turn reshape water availability and risk. Within this broader
perspective, our Sri Lanka research builds the hydrological foundation needed to understand
these interactions by examining how seasonal rainfall regimes propagate through soil moisture
and runoff across different regions and timescales. We decompose Sri Lanka’s rainfall into
interpretable seasonal patterns and investigate how present and antecedent rainfall influence
water stored at different soil depths, before extending the framework toward surface and
subsurface runoff. The long-term aim is to connect these climate–water dynamics with human
water use and management, supporting more resilient decision-making under Sri Lanka’s strong
seasonal and regional variability.
