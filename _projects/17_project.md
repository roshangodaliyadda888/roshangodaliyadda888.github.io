## Overview

This project aims to develop and validate a multimodal artificial intelligence model that can
predict recovery and psychological outcomes in patients following acute ischemic stroke.
Patients admitted within 24 hours of stroke onset will be prospectively followed for 12 months.
The model will combine information from four complementary sources: baseline clinical
characteristics, whole-brain non-contrast CT images, quantitative EEG measurements, and
objective movement data collected using an in-house monitoring device. These data will be
used to predict functional outcomes such as the modified Rankin Scale, NIHSS, and Barthel
Index, as well as post-stroke depressive symptoms measured using the Beck Depression
Inventory at 3, 6, and 12 months. The main objective is to determine whether combining these
different types of information can provide more accurate and comprehensive predictions of
individual recovery than conventional clinical information alone. The study will also examine the
contribution of each modality and develop an interpretable model that could eventually support
personalized rehabilitation planning and post-stroke care.
